zSwap

zSwap is a DEX on Ethereum, Base and Robinhood Chain. It compares onchain venues and outside aggregators, then settles the best route through one router. The app itself is stored on Ethereum.

TradeSwap, place orders and send. Provide liquidityEarn fees in a Precision pool band. BuildRead, quote and trade Precision pools from your own code.

Quick start

  1. Open the app at zswap.wei.limo, or any gateway under Onchain hosting.
  2. Connect a wallet. Browser wallets are detected automatically. Phone wallets connect by QR code.
  3. Pick a network with the network icon in the top right.
  4. Choose tokens and an amount. The best route appears. Tap ▾ next to the rate to compare alternatives.
  5. Confirm. The app simulates the transaction, then your wallet asks you to sign.
Use your own nodeNetwork icon → "Read node". Paste any https RPC URL and the app reads through it first. Signing always stays in your wallet.

Swap

Type an amount in either box. The pay box quotes exact input; the receive box quotes exact output.

Where prices come from

ChainOnchain venues
EthereumUniswap V2/V3/V4, SushiSwap, Curve, Lido, Tacit, Precision pools, zSwap orders
BaseUniswap V2/V3/V4, Aerodrome, Slipstream, Precision pools, zSwap orders
RobinhoodUniswap V2/V3/V4, Deepstate order book, Precision pools, zSwap orders

Routes can be one hop, pass through a hub token, or split across venues. Each settles in one transaction, with ETH wrapped or unwrapped as needed. zSwap orders join quotes once a wallet is connected.

How routes are built

Routing is done by onchain contracts the app calls with eth_call, so every candidate is ready-to-send router calldata:

  • zQuoter prices each AMM on the chain and builds the best single hop, two- and three-hop routes through hub tokens, a split of the input across the top two venues, and a hybrid of a direct route with a hub route.
  • Order-book plans fill resting asks and bids and take any remainder from a zQuoter route, settled together by Swapbol in one call.
  • Precision routes walk one or more Precision pools through PrecisionRoute.

The app compares these with the aggregators and sends the best through zRouter, which checks what you receive.

Aggregators

The app also asks 0x, 1inch, ParaSwap, KyberSwap, Enso and OKX on all three chains, and Bebop on Ethereum and Base. The list, and the chains each one serves, are read from the onchain registry zSolverList 0x1Dfbb2…A2e3.

  • Quotes are compared net of your slippage.
  • An aggregator must beat the best onchain route by its handicap, set in the registry (0.5% today).
  • A winning aggregator still settles through zRouter, which checks the amount you receive.
  • Aggregators never see your wallet address. Slow (over 1.5 s) or failing ones are skipped.

Orders

Send

Send to a 0x address or a name (.wei, .eth, .base.eth).

  • SLOW sends are time-locked and can be reversed until they unlock.
  • Ethereum → Base or Robinhood uses the chain's own bridge. Only ETH crosses.
  • Out of Base or Robinhood uses a relayer (off by default, under "Another chain"). If no relayer fills, you can reclaim after 8 days 6 hours.

Precision pools

Overview

Precision pools are zSwap's own AMM. Each pool trades one pair inside one price band, and the pool contract is also its LP token.

One bandper pool, fixed at creation
ERC-20 LP"Precision LP" (pLP), with permit and Permit2
Auto-compoundingfees stay in the pool; nothing to claim
No ownerno pause, no upgrade, no protocol fee
  • Fees compound automatically. Each trade's LP fee is added to the pool, so your pLP grows in value with no claiming, restaking or gas.
  • Simple LP. Everyone in a pool holds the same position. No NFTs, no ticks.
  • Concentrated. All liquidity sits inside the band, so trades there get deeper liquidity.
  • Native ETH. ETH is held as ETH, not WETH.
  • Price history built in. Every pool records its own candles onchain.
  • Same addresses everywhere. The factory, lenses, zap, hook, policy and launcher share one address on all three chains. PrecisionRoute has one on Ethereum and another on Base and Robinhood.

How it fits together

Precision pools and the order books are the two kinds of liquidity zSwap runs itself. Pools give passive liquidity across a band, and LPs earn the fees. Boards hold orders at prices their makers choose. For every swap the app quotes both, alongside other venues, and the best route settles through zRouter in one transaction. One order-book fill can also take its remainder from other AMMs (Uniswap, Curve and others, routed by zQuoter) in the same transaction. See How routes are built.

How a band works

A band has a low and a high price. Inside it, the pool holds both tokens and earns fees on every trade. As the price moves, the pool sells the token that is rising and buys the other one. At the edges it holds one token only.

  • A trade that would push the price past an edge is refused. Routes can instead fill up to the edge and send the rest elsewhere.
  • A wider band covers more prices but is shallower, so it earns less per dollar deposited. A narrower band earns more while the price stays inside, and stops earning sooner.

The math

The pool prices trades as a constant-product AMM on virtual reserves: its real reserves plus the liquidity that would sit outside the band.

X = r0 + L / √pHigh
Y = r1 + L · √pLow
out = in′ · Y / (X + in′)

r0, r1: real reserves. L: the pool's LP supply. in′: input after fees. Prices are token1 per token0, where token0 is the lower address and ETH is always token0. Onchain, square-root prices are scaled by 1018 in raw token units. Because fees stay in the reserves, a pool at an edge can still hold a little of the other token: earned fees, owned by LPs.

Fees

  • Base fee: chosen at creation and fixed. The app offers 0.05% (pairs that track each other), 0.30% (most pairs) and 1.00% (thin or volatile).
  • LP earnings compound. The LP part of the fee stays in the pool, so each pLP is worth more over time. There is nothing to claim.
  • Creator share and hook surcharge exist only if the pool was created with them; see Creator fees & hooks. Pools created in the app have neither.
  • No protocol fee, and no router fee.

For liquidity providers

Guide for LPs

Open the app, choose Liquidity from the menu, and pick the two tokens. Each band for that pair is listed with a bar showing where the price sits in it.

How you earn

Every trade inside your band pays the pool's fee, and the LP part goes straight into the pool's reserves. Your share of the pool grows with it, and those fees also deepen the pool for the next trade. There is nothing to claim or restake, and compounding costs no gas. Uniswap V3 and V4 positions collect fees separately, so you have to claim and re-deposit them yourself.

Add to a band

  1. Choose a band whose bar shows the price inside it.
  2. Enter amounts of both tokens. Whatever doesn't fit the pool's ratio is refunded.
  3. Or tick one-sided to deposit one token. The app swaps part of it in the same transaction, and reverts if you'd get fewer pLP than quoted minus slippage. Pools with a hook need both tokens.

Create a new band

  1. Under the list, choose Create a new band.
  2. Pick a range: Full range (the widest band your deposit supports), Wide 100×, 10×, 2×, Tight 25%, or custom prices. "10×" means from a tenth of the opening price to ten times it when you deposit both tokens. With one token, the band starts at the opening price and extends 10× in one direction.
  3. Pick a fee: 0.05%, 0.30% or 1.00%.
  4. Enter amounts. Their ratio sets the opening price. For a one-token deposit, type the opening price. Prices are shown as the second token per the first (the lower address); a band of only the first token sits above the opening price, and one of only the second sits below it.
  5. Preview, then create. The new pool has no owner, no fee recipient and no hook, and anyone can add to it.

Remove

Take 25%, 50%, 75% or all of a position. You get your share of both tokens at the current mix. Exits work at any price.

Risks

  • Price moves. As the price crosses your band you end up holding more of the token that fell (impermanent loss).
  • Out of range. Outside the band your position holds one token and earns nothing until the price returns.
  • Contracts and tokens. Pools can't be changed, but they can have bugs. Tokens that charge a transfer fee aren't supported.
  • Permit2. pLP gives the canonical Permit2 contract a standing allowance, like many newer tokens. Moving your pLP through it still needs your signature.

TAC/ETH farm

Stake LP from the TAC/ETH pool 0x015535…3D6E in the PrecisionFarm 0x000000…82E0 to earn TAC on top of pool fees. Open it from the menu (Farm) or with #farm.

  • Stake, unstake, claim. Unstaking everything also claims. Claim collects TAC without unstaking.
  • Zaps: start from ETH or TAC to add liquidity and stake in one transaction. Exit as ETH and TAC, ETH only, TAC only, or LP.
  • Rewards stream at a set rate while the farm holds TAC. Anyone can top it up. The owner (Tacit's governance multisig) can change the rate but can't touch staked LP or rewards already scheduled.

On October 25, 2026: 553.89 TAC per day, funded through December 19, 2026. Read rewardRate() and periodFinish() for current values.

Price charts

Every pool stores its own candles onchain, in two rings of 256 bars. The app's 5m, 1h, 4h and 1d charts read them directly.

Each bar holds open, high, low, close, volume and trade count, at the executed price with fees included. The tape is not an oracle: a single trade can move it, so don't rely on it where manipulation matters.

Creator fees & hooks

Elsewhere, the most common hook pays the pool's creator. Precision pools build that in. Hooks stay optional and can only add a capped fee.

Built-in creator fees

  • Set at creation with two fields of the pool's Market: feeRecipient and creatorFeeBps, the creator's share of the base fee (up to 5000, i.e. 50%). Both are fixed for the pool's life.
  • Traders pay the same fee. The creator share comes out of the base fee, and LPs keep the rest.
  • No extra contract and no external call per trade.
  • Claiming: the share builds up in whichever token was paid in. The fee recipient claims both tokens with collectCreatorFees(to).
  • Protected opening. A pool with a fee recipient can only be created and seeded through the factory by that fee recipient, so nobody else can set its opening price.
  • Coins launched in the app use this. Each launch pool's creator share goes to the launcher, which splits it between the coin's creator, the protocol and a tithe.

What a hook can do

A hook is optional, named when the pool is created, and fixed. The pool calls it in two places:

CallWhenLimits
feeFor(sender, tokenIn, amountIn)Before each trade, read-onlyReturns an extra fee. Capped at 150,000 gas. A failure counts as no extra fee. Base fee plus surcharge can't exceed 10%.
afterSwap(…)After each tradeA notification only. Capped at 150,000 gas. A failure is logged and the trade still completes.

A hook can't change the price curve, block or reverse a trade, move the pool's funds or touch liquidity. Its surcharge is held in the pool and paid only to the hook.

Hooked pools give up a few things. They can't be partly filled (swapUpTo), the app's one-sided add isn't available for them, and zSwap won't route through them unless the routing policy approves the pool. Their quotes can also change if the hook's fee changes.

ConstantSurchargeHook

The live hook 0x000000…ec03 is one contract shared by every pool that names it. It charges a flat surcharge controlled by each pool's fee recipient.

  • Cuts take effect immediately.
  • Increases must be announced. They can apply after 1 day, anyone can apply them during the next 7 days, and after that they lapse.
  • Anyone can call collect(pool), which pays the pool's fee recipient. The fee recipient can also send it elsewhere with collectTo.

Routing policy

PrecisionPoolPolicy 0x000000…EC6D says which pools routers should use: pools without a hook by default, plus hooked pools it approves, minus any pool it blocks. The zSwap app follows it when quoting. It doesn't stop anyone trading with a pool directly.

Compared with Uniswap V4 hooks

Anyone can deploy a contract that looks like a pool. Only trust addresses the factory created.

4. Swap through zRouter

The recommended path. zRouter moves your input to PrecisionRoute, which walks the pools, and the router reverts unless the recipient's balance of tokenOut rose by at least minOut. PrecisionRoute only accepts calls from the router's executor, so always go through snwap.

interface IZRouter {
  function snwap(address tokenIn, uint256 amountIn, address recipient, address tokenOut,
                 uint256 amountOutMin, address executor, bytes calldata executorData)
    external payable returns (uint256 amountOut);
  function multicall(bytes[] calldata data) external payable returns (bytes[] memory);
}
interface IPrecisionRoute {
  function route(address[] calldata pools, address tokenIn, address tokenOut,
                 uint256 amountIn, uint256 minOut, address to) external payable returns (uint256);
  function checkpoint(address token, bytes32 intent, address refundTo) external;
}

ETH in: one call, sending the ETH with it.

bytes memory r = abi.encodeCall(IPrecisionRoute.route,
  (pools, address(0), tokenOut, amountIn, minOut, to));
IZRouter(ZROUTER).snwap{value: amountIn}(address(0), amountIn, to, tokenOut, minOut, PROUTE, r);

Token in: approve zRouter for amountIn, then a multicall of two legs. The first commits PrecisionRoute to the exact route calldata, the second funds and runs it.

bytes memory r = abi.encodeCall(IPrecisionRoute.route,
  (pools, tokenIn, tokenOut, amountIn, minOut, to));
bytes[] memory legs = new bytes[](2);
legs[0] = abi.encodeCall(IZRouter.snwap, (address(0), 0, to, address(0), 0, PROUTE,
  abi.encodeCall(IPrecisionRoute.checkpoint, (tokenIn, keccak256(r), refundTo))));
legs[1] = abi.encodeCall(IZRouter.snwap, (tokenIn, amountIn, to, tokenOut, minOut, PROUTE, r));
IZRouter(ZROUTER).multicall(legs);

Deadline: neither the pools nor PrecisionRoute check time. Put a zGuard leg first in the multicall. It reverts after by and returns any ETH to the router, so the next leg can still spend it.

bytes memory guard = abi.encodeCall(IZRouter.snwap, (address(0), 0, to, address(0), 0, ZGUARD,
  abi.encodeWithSignature("deadline(uint256,address)", block.timestamp + 600, ZROUTER)));
// ETH in:   multicall{value: amountIn} with legs [guard, route leg]
// token in: multicall with legs [guard, checkpoint leg, route leg]

Other entry points on PrecisionRoute, called the same way: routeUpTo(pools, tokenIn, tokenOut, amountIn, minOut, to, refundTo) fills as much as every hop can take and refunds the rest in tokenIn (pools without a hook only); routeFromWETH(pools, tokenOut, amountIn, minOut, to) takes WETH into an ETH pool.

5. Swap a pool directly

// ETH in: send exactly amountIn as msg.value. Token in: approve the pool first.
pool.swapExactIn(address tokenIn, uint256 amountIn, uint256 minOut, address to)
  payable returns (uint256 amountOut)

// fills up to the band edge and refunds the rest (pools without a hook only)

Cheapest path for a contract that already holds the input. minOut is the only guard; add your own deadline check.

7. Create a pool

struct Market {
  address token0;        // lower address; address(0) for ETH
  address token1;
  uint256 sqrtPLow;      // sqrt(price) × 1e18, see below
  uint256 sqrtPHigh;
  uint256 fee;           // pips: 3000 = 0.30%; must be below 100000 (10%)
  address hook;          // address(0) for none
  address feeRecipient;  // address(0) for an unowned pool
  uint256 creatorFeeBps; // feeRecipient's share of the base fee, max 5000
}

factory.createAndSeed(Market m, uint256 sqrtPriceInit, uint256 amount0,
                      uint256 amount1, uint256 minLP, address to) payable
  returns (address pool, uint256 lp, uint256 used0, uint256 used1)

factory.createPool(Market m) returns (address pool)        // deploy only
factory.seed(Market m, sqrtPriceInit, amount0, amount1, minLP, to) payable  // first deposit
factory.poolFor(Market m) view returns (address)           // address before creation
// a price, as token1 per token0 in whole units, to the onchain form
sqrtP = sqrt(price × 10^(decimals1 − decimals0)) × 1e18
  • Approve the factory for token inputs, and send ETH as msg.value.
  • The first deposit sets the opening price (sqrtPriceInit, inside the band). A deposit at an edge is one-sided.
  • 1,000 LP units from the first deposit are locked forever.
  • A pool with a feeRecipient must be created and seeded by that address, so call the factory from the contract or wallet that will receive fees.
  • Each Market maps to one address, so the same pool can't be created twice.

8. Collect creator fees

pool.creatorOwed0() / pool.creatorOwed1()             // accrued, per token
pool.collectCreatorFees(address to) returns (uint256 amount0, uint256 amount1)  // feeRecipient only

Both tokens are paid together. feeRecipient and the share are fixed, so a contract that needs to hand fees on, like the launcher, should be the fee recipient and forward them itself.

9. Hooks

Use the shared surcharge hook: create the pool with hook = HOOK and a feeRecipient (the share can be 0). The fee recipient then controls the surcharge, in pips:

hook.surchargeOf(pool)                          // current surcharge
hook.setSurcharge(pool, pips)                   // lower it, immediately
hook.scheduleSurchargeIncrease(pool, pips)      // raise it: usable after 1 day
hook.applySurchargeIncrease(pool)               // anyone, within 7 days after that
hook.cancelSurchargeIncrease(pool)
hook.collect(pool)                              // anyone; pays the pool's feeRecipient
hook.collectTo(pool, to)                        // feeRecipient only

Write your own: any contract with these two functions can be a pool's hook.

interface IPrecisionHook {
  // extra fee in pips (1e6 = 100%); capped so base + extra ≤ 10%
  function feeFor(address sender, address tokenIn, uint256 amountIn)
    external view returns (uint256 extraFee);
  // notification after each swap; failures are ignored
  function afterSwap(address sender, address tokenIn, uint256 amountIn,
                     uint256 amountOut, address to) external;
}

pool.hookOwed0() / pool.hookOwed1()
pool.collectHookFees(address to)   // callable only by the hook
  • Each call gets at most 150,000 gas. A feeFor that fails or answers badly means no surcharge.
  • Compute the fee from state the pool exposes (reserves, supply, the price tape), not from counters kept in afterSwap: a caller can starve that callback, and the trade still succeeds.
  • Hooked pools can't be partly filled or zapped, and zSwap only routes them if the routing policy approves them.

11. Read candles

pool.tape(300, 256)       // 5-minute bars, newest first; 0 = empty bar

Good to know

  • Pools pull input with transferFrom and check the exact amount arrived, so tokens with transfer fees revert.
  • ETH sent to a pool outside a swap or deposit reverts.
  • Pools have no owner, pause or upgrade. Everything in Market is fixed for the pool's life.
  • Solidity 0.8.36, EVM target Prague (transient storage), MIT license. Source: src/pools/PrecisionPool.sol and the rest of src/pools. PrecisionOraclePool, PrecisionRangePool and PrecisionStablePool are research references and aren't deployed.

Launch

A coin

One transaction, no ETH beyond gas. The PrecisionLauncher 0x000000…3aEe mints a fixed supply. You keep up to 20%; the rest opens a one-sided ETH Precision pool. The logo is stored onchain (the creator can replace it later), and the launcher keeps the pool's LP so liquidity can't be pulled.

  • Launched pools charge 1% per trade. Half stays in the pool. The other half is collectable and split 80% to the creator, 10% to the protocol, 10% to a tithe.
  • The token side of collected fees is burned. The tithe's ETH goes to the BETH burner address; on Ethereum that also records a BETH receipt.
  • Holders can always redeem coins for their share of the ETH in the launch pool. Developers: see Launch a coin.

A cause (Ethereum)

A cause is a small DAO with a funding goal in ETH. Backers buy units (non-voting loot), and the treasury is released to the creator over the time you set. Backers can burn their units at any time for their share of what hasn't been released. The creator holds the only vote and can turn that refund off, but only after a 3-day public delay that leaves backers time to burn.

Names

Register .wei names on Ethereum (WNS 0x000000…42EB). Paid names start at 0.0005 ETH per year and take two steps, at least 60 seconds apart. Free name.id.wei names take one. Address fields across the app accept .wei, .eth and .base.eth names.

Markets

Yes/no prediction markets on PM 0x000000…B5C5 (Ethereum). Winners split the losing side's pot.

  • Pots in ETH, wstETH, USDC, BOLD or tacUSD.
  • Each market names a resolver you trust, with a fee of at most 10% fixed at creation.
  • A market is void if a side is empty at resolution or the resolver voids it, and anyone can void one left unresolved 30 days after close. Each share then takes an equal part of the pot, with no fee.

Private

Private payments through Tacit: shield, send privately to a tacit1 address or a name, and withdraw. Ethereum has the full set, including cBTC and cUSD; Base and Robinhood have the private ETH pool.

  • Your Tacit key comes from one wallet signature per visit and is never stored.
  • A withdrawal's amount, recipient and timing are public.

Points

Activity in zSwap, such as ETH swaps and ETH prediction bets, can earn points in Tacit's program. Your points show under the swap box once a wallet is connected, and are claimed on Ethereum. Tacit sets and changes the rules; see tacit.finance.

For developers

zRouter

zRouter executes every zSwap trade. It has the same address on all three chains, with a build per chain for that chain's venues, and charges no fee. Calls are small typed legs (one venue each) that you chain in a multicall; transient balances pass each leg's output to the next.

// Ethereum:  swapVZ (zAMM), swapCurve, exactETHToSTETH / exactETHToWSTETH,
//            ethToExactSTETH / ethToExactWSTETH
// Base:      swapAero(to, stable, tokenIn, tokenOut, swapAmount, amountLimit, deadline)
//            swapAeroCL(to, exactOut, tickSpacing, tokenIn, tokenOut, swapAmount, amountLimit, deadline)
// Robinhood: swapDeep(to, token0, token1, epoch, order, isBid, amountInMax, amountOutMin, deadline)
  • address(0) means ETH. It is wrapped or unwrapped for the venue automatically.
  • Exact input (exactOut = false): swapAmount is what you pay and amountLimit the minimum you receive. Exact output: swapAmount is what you want and amountLimit the most you'll pay.
  • Every leg checks its own deadline, and returns (amountIn, amountOut).

Chaining with transient balances

Each leg records its output for its recipient in transient storage (EIP-1153), which lasts only for the transaction. When the recipient is the router, the next leg spends that credit instead of pulling from your wallet, so a multi-hop route is just a list of legs:

  • Send a leg's output to the router (to = ZROUTER) to keep it for the next leg.
  • On an exact-input leg, swapAmount = 0 spends everything the router holds of tokenIn (or msg.value for ETH), so you don't need to know the previous leg's output in advance.
  • deposit pre-funds the router once from your wallet, and permit2TransferFrom does the same with a Permit2 signature. Later legs spend it.
  • sweep(token, amount, to) sends leftovers out (amount = 0 sends everything). Add it after exact-output legs to return unspent input.
interface IZRouter {
  function swapV3(address to, bool exactOut, uint24 swapFee, address tokenIn, address tokenOut,
                  uint256 swapAmount, uint256 amountLimit, uint256 deadline)
    external payable returns (uint256 amountIn, uint256 amountOut);
  function multicall(bytes[] calldata data) external payable returns (bytes[] memory);
}

bytes[] memory legs = new bytes[](2);
// 1. 0.01 ETH -> USDC on Uniswap V3 (0.05%), kept in the router
legs[0] = abi.encodeCall(IZRouter.swapV3,
  (ZROUTER, false, 500, address(0), USDC, 0.01 ether, 0, deadline));
// 2. all of that USDC -> DAI on Uniswap V3 (0.01%), paid to the caller
legs[1] = abi.encodeCall(IZRouter.swapV3,
  (msg.sender, false, 100, USDC, DAI, 0, minDai, deadline));
IZRouter(ZROUTER).multicall{value: 0.01 ether}(legs);

Simulated on Ethereum on October 14, 2026: 0.01 ETH → 24.32 USDC → 24.31 DAI. swapAmount = 0 reads the router's whole balance of the token, including anything someone else left there, so size legs explicitly when that matters.

snwap: any executor

snwap(tokenIn, amountIn, recipient, tokenOut, amountOutMin, executor, executorData) sends your input to executor, runs executorData through a SafeExecutor that holds no approvals, then reverts unless recipient's balance of tokenOut rose by at least amountOutMin. zSwap uses it for aggregator fills, PrecisionRoute, the order books and zGuard. See Swap through zRouter for a worked example.

Funding

  • ETH: send it as msg.value.
  • Tokens: approve the router for the amount, or put permit(token, value, deadline, v, r, s) (EIP-2612) or permit2TransferFrom(token, amount, nonce, deadline, signature) first in the multicall. Ethereum also has permitDAI and permit2BatchTransferFrom.
  • Quotes: zQuoter 0x000000…Bf9B returns ready-to-send legs (buildBestSwap, buildBestSwapViaETHMulticall, buildSplitSwap, buildHybridSplit) for the same address on each chain.

Upgrades

A version never changes. An upgrade is a new version at a new address, linked to the last one.

  • Only the zFi DAO 0x5E58BA…053E can add a version to the lineage (v0.1 → v0.2 → …), once per version, and the new one must answer to the same DAO. Anyone can deploy a standalone copy, but it isn't part of the lineage.
  • zSwapResolver 0x000000…b7AC only serves a new version once it has stood for 3 days. A name pointed at the resolver gets that delay; a name its owner sets directly does not.
  • Members vote, queue and execute in the app's governance panel (footer → governance).
